OPEN THE MODEL

Curiosity first.
Certainty never.

This project begins with a question from The Great Derangement: why is it so hard to imagine the scale of climate change? Our model makes its assumptions visible, so the demonstration can be about thinking—not just numbers.

01
Choose a place

Each city has a fictionalized teaching profile describing broad sensitivities. The profile values are illustrative, not sourced measurements.

02
Set a scenario

Select a warming level, rainfall assumption and time horizon. These are classroom controls, not official climate pathways.

03
Notice the signals

Simple formulas combine profile values and scenario settings into 0–100 pressure scores. Actions reduce selected scores by fixed demonstration amounts.

04
Ask what could change

Use the result to discuss adaptation and justice. The model does not establish what will happen or which policy will work.

TRANSPARENCY

Data, assumptions & limits

What data does this use?

No live climate observations or measured city-risk datasets are included. City profile numbers are invented teaching inputs, chosen to demonstrate how a transparent calculation behaves. Coordinates are used only to identify cities and are approximate.

What are the formulas?

Each pressure starts from a category-specific base, adds a weighted illustrative city-profile value, then adds the scenario effects. Scores are rounded and clamped from 0 to 100. The source code in backend/server.py contains every coefficient.

What do adaptation actions do?

Shade, drainage and wetland options subtract fixed amounts from selected indicators. These are hypothetical examples to prompt discussion, not measured intervention impacts or cost-benefit analysis.

What should I say in a presentation?

“These values are not predictions. They are a transparent classroom model showing how assumptions affect an output. A real analysis would need verified local data, validated methods and expert review.”

Suggested future sources

Replace the sample inputs only after reviewing official sources such as India Meteorological Department publications, city disaster management plans, and IPCC assessment material. This demo does not claim that any such source supplied its current values.
